# Docstoc.com — External Backlinks & Mentions

## Summary

Found 38 distinct external mentions/references to docstoc.com across news coverage, tech blogs, forums, review sites, academic citations, and "alternative"/comparison pages. The clearest pattern: a wave of 2007–2009 tech/lifestyle blog posts introduced docstoc.com as a "YouTube for documents" and almost all of those posts embedded a genuine, still-live hyperlink to `http://www.docstoc.com` in their opening paragraph — several of these (calculatedmoves.com, flippingheck.com, CBS News, SoCalTech, TechCrunch, johnchow.com) still have that link live today, pointing straight into the dead GoDaddy parking page. A second wave, from Sept–Dec 2015, covered the shutdown itself (TechCrunch "Goodbye Docstoc," LA Business Journal, Digital Reader, Clausehound, socaltech) — these mostly reference Docstoc by name without a live hyperlink, since by then the destination was already dying. A third cluster is evergreen "alternatives"/"competitors" landing pages (DocHub, PDFLiner, sitelike.org, topsimilarsites.com, moreofit.com, CB Insights, Owler) that still rank today for "docstoc alternative" type queries and are either static from ~2013 or actively maintained SEO comparison pages built around the docstoc name as a keyword magnet. A fourth cluster is complaint/review sites (Ripoff Report, MouthShut, ComplaintsBoard, SmartCustomer, Scamadviser) documenting billing complaints from the paid-membership era. Academic/.edu citations (NDU, Berkeley, Cornell, Indiana University) point to docstoc.com/docs/ style deep document URLs that are now 404/parked — these are low-value for outreach but illustrate the deep-link decay pattern relevant to a reclamation strategy targeting `docstoc.com/docs/*` deep links specifically. No Reddit threads discussing docstoc surfaced (search results returned only unrelated Wikipedia/TechCrunch noise); Quora and forum coverage (ProZ, Warrior Forum, India Broadband) was thin but real, focused on SEO/link-building use of docstoc as a document-sharing/backlink tactic circa 2009–2011.

## Patterns & observations

- **The single most actionable cluster is early blog reviews (2007–2009)** that introduced Docstoc as a novel "document sharing" concept and hyperlinked it in their opening paragraph. Several of these (calculatedmoves.com, flippingheck.com, CBS News, SoCalTech, johnchow.com, TechCrunch) still carry that link untouched, 10+ years after it went dead — these are the highest-ROI backlink-reclamation targets since the link already exists and the page already covers "document templates" as a topic; the ask is simply "please update this now-dead link to [Chasa/Docracy resource]."
- **Shutdown-announcement coverage (Sept–Dec 2015)** almost never links out to docstoc.com itself (understandably, since the point of the article is that it's closing) — this cluster is good for citation/context but not for direct link reclamation. It is, however, a strong model set for a Chasa/Docracy content piece along the lines of "Docstoc is gone — here's where to find free legal & business templates now," explicitly targeting the residual search demand these articles still generate.
- **"Docstoc alternative"/"sites like docstoc" is a live, contested keyword cluster.** DocHub, PDFLiner, pdfFiller, CB Insights, Owler, sitelike.org, topsimilarsites.com, and moreofit.com all maintain pages targeting this exact query today (some claim 2025 updates), which is strong evidence of durable organic search demand more than a decade after the shutdown. This is arguably more valuable to Chasa than backlink reclamation alone: it suggests building an owned "docstoc alternative" / "free legal templates since docstoc shut down" landing page, plus pitching inclusion in the DocHub/PDFLiner/CB Insights-style listicles.
- **Stale automated directories (topsimilarsites.com, sitelike.org) still carry live outbound links** to docstoc.com sourced from data frozen around 2013 — these are low-effort, low-authority targets but essentially "free" reclamation if the sites accept update submissions.
- **Academic/.edu citations** (NDU, Berkeley, Cornell, Indiana University, Academia.edu) point to specific `docstoc.com/docs/...` deep-link documents that Docstoc hosted as a quasi-CDN for primary sources (white papers, government documents, research). These links are now uniformly dead, but they illustrate that docstoc.com accrued authoritative inbound citations at the individual-document level, not just the homepage level — a reclamation strategy focused only on the root domain will miss this long tail of deep-link decay.
- **Review/complaint sites (Ripoff Report, MouthShut, ComplaintsBoard, SmartCustomer, Scamadviser) form a distinct negative-sentiment cluster**, almost entirely about the paid membership/DocCash monetization era's billing practices. Not useful for backlink reclamation, but useful competitive-positioning ammunition ("no surprise billing, no gimmicks") if Chasa/Docracy wants to draw an implicit contrast in marketing copy.
- **Forum chatter (Warrior Forum, ProZ, India Broadband) is thin but consistent**: marketers used Docstoc circa 2009–2011 as a document-based SEO/traffic/AdSense-revenue-share tactic, until Docstoc disabled live links inside document previews, which forum members loudly complained about and treated as the beginning of the platform's decline for SEO purposes. This is useful competitive intel: it shows a historical playbook (publish useful documents on a widely-trafficked site, embed backlinks, share AdSense) that Chasa could adapt today via its own template marketplace rather than a third party.
- **No genuine Reddit discussion threads surfaced** despite repeated query attempts — Reddit's index for "docstoc" appears to be dominated by unrelated crossposts/noise in these search results, suggesting either very little historical Reddit discussion of the brand or poor searchability; worth a manual Reddit-native search (via Reddit's own search) as a follow-up if Reddit community sentiment specifically is wanted.
- **Several fetches were blocked (403 Forbidden)**: ProZ.com forum thread, successful-blog.com review, and adamsdrafting.com contract-drafting review of Docstoc all returned 403s to the fetch tool and could not be verified for live-link status — these would need manual browser verification.

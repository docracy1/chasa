Compliance Certificate

Free compliance certificate template — a borrower confirms to a lender that loan covenants are currently being met.

Certify a document you draft →
  • SSL-secured
  • No signup required
  • Free, always

Drafting a compliance certificate from scratch is slow, and generic templates often miss the sections that actually matter. This free template is built for small businesses and individuals documenting a loan or expense putting financial terms in writing so both sides have a clear, shared record. It's structured with the standard sections a document like this needs, with clearly marked [placeholder] fields so you can fill in your own details quickly. Copy it into your own word processor, fill in the placeholders, and review it — or adapt it — before use. It's part of docstoc's free document template library, alongside business, legal, real estate, finance, and HR templates. Every template here is free to copy with no signup required, and each one carries a plain disclaimer: this is a starting point for informational purposes, not a substitute for advice from a licensed professional in your jurisdiction.

Compliance Certificate

Borrower: [Company Name] Lender: [Bank/Lender Name] Loan Agreement: dated [Date] Reporting Period: [Period] Date: [Date]

The undersigned, an authorized officer of Borrower, certifies as follows:

1. Financial Covenants

CovenantRequiredActualCompliant?
[e.g. Debt-to-EBITDA ratio][Requirement][Actual][Yes/No]
[e.g. Minimum current ratio][Requirement][Actual][Yes/No]

2. No Default

Borrower is not aware of any event of default, or event that with notice or the passage of time would become an event of default, under the Loan Agreement.

3. Financial Statements

The attached financial statements are true and correct and prepared in accordance with [GAAP/applicable standard].

Certified by: ______________________ Date: ____________ [Name, Title, Borrower]

*Most loan agreements require this certificate on a set schedule (quarterly is common) — mark your calendar, since missing the deadline to certify can itself sometimes trigger a technical default under the loan terms.*

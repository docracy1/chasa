Chasa vs Tugboat Logic — audit prep vs. automated evidence collection

Tugboat Logic, now part of OneTrust, helps companies get organized for SOC2 and ISO27001 audits: policy templates, control mapping, and security-questionnaire workflows, sold on a custom, quote-based basis. It's a solid tool for organizing what you already need to prove — but the underlying work of collecting the actual evidence still falls largely on your team. Chasa is a much smaller, AR-focused product with transparent flat pricing from $7/mo, and it's making a different bet: automate the evidence collection itself, not just the paperwork around it. That's already true for accounts receivable — every chase drafted in Chasa is timestamped into an audit trail auditors can use today (see SOX evidence automation). Extending that same automation to broader SaaS evidence is our roadmap, described transparently at /security — not a feature we're claiming to have shipped.

FAQ

Is Chasa a Tugboat Logic alternative?

Not a full one today. Tugboat Logic, now part of OneTrust, helps companies organize security questionnaires, policy templates, and audit prep across frameworks like SOC2 and ISO27001. Chasa is a much smaller, AR-focused product: a live AI invoice-chase tool with a genuine audit trail for accounts-receivable activity today, and a roadmap to build faster, more automated evidence collection for broader SaaS evidence.

How much does Tugboat Logic cost compared to Chasa?

Tugboat Logic is sold through OneTrust on a custom, quote-based basis — check onetrust.com directly for current pricing. Chasa publishes transparent flat pricing starting at $7/mo for Solo.

Does Chasa automate evidence collection like Tugboat Logic?

Not fully yet. Chasa is not SOC2 or ISO27001 certified today and hasn't built the broad SaaS evidence connectors that Tugboat/OneTrust offer — that's on our public roadmap at /security. What's live today is evidence automation for accounts receivable: a timestamped chase and activity trail auditors can use for AR testing now, described at /use-cases/sox-evidence-automation.

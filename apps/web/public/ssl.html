Product / SSL Automation

Free SSL, Without Running Your Own ACME Client

Let's Encrypt certificates are free — but issuing one still means standing up an ACME client, or trusting a third-party tool with the process. docstoc handles the ACME order for you: add a domain, add one DNS record, get a real, trusted certificate.

How it works

  • Add the domain: No CLI, no server access — just the domain name.
  • Add one TXT record: docstoc gives you the DNS-01 challenge value for your DNS provider.
  • Get a real certificate: Once DNS propagates, docstoc completes the ACME order against Let's Encrypt's production directory — not a staging/test cert.
  • Renewal reminders: An email before it expires, with a one-click re-verify — see SSL monitoring.

A verified trust profile comes with it

Small businesses often struggle to look credible against larger vendors. The moment your first domain's certificate is issued — real proof of DNS control — docstoc creates a "verified since" record for your account, anchors that claim to Bitcoin, and gives you an embeddable badge to put on your own site or proposals.   • What it actually verifies: DNS control of a domain and, once Bitcoin-confirmed, the date your account first reached that status — SSL status shown is always a live check, never frozen.
  • What it doesn't claim: business registration or legal entity status — docstoc doesn't check business registries, so the badge never says it does.
  • Automatic: no separate signup — it's created the moment your first domain goes active.

SSL Automation FAQ

Is the certificate actually issued by Let's Encrypt, or by docstoc?

By Let's Encrypt directly — docstoc completes the ACME protocol exchange on your behalf, but the certificate authority is Let's Encrypt's own production directory, the same one any ACME client talks to.

Do I need to install anything?

No client, no CLI. The only manual step is pasting one DNS TXT record into your domain's DNS provider — everything else happens in the docstoc app.

What if I already have a certificate from somewhere else?

You can add the domain to docstoc and issue a new certificate independently — it doesn't need to know about or replace an existing certificate from another provider.

Certificate lifetimes are getting shorter. That is not a vendor upsell — it is a deliberate industry shift to limit how long a stolen private key remains useful. This guide explains what is changing, why 90-day (and shorter) certs exist, and how small businesses should respond without living in calendar reminders.

Why lifetimes are shrinking

Long-lived certificates (years) were convenient and dangerous: a compromised key could impersonate your site until expiry or revocation (revocation is imperfect on the public web). Shorter lifetimes force fresher keys and make automation the default. Let's Encrypt’s widely deployed ~90-day DV certificates trained the internet on that model.

What is changing in practice

  • Baseline public TLS lifetimes have already dropped from multi-year norms toward about one year maximum for many public certs — with free ACME CAs much shorter.
  • Further reductions (including proposals measured in tens of days) continue to appear in CA/Browser Forum discussions and CA roadmaps. Exact dates shift; the direction does not.
  • Short-lived certificates only work if renewal is automatic. Manual CSR workflows break.

Impact on freelancers and SMBs

If you buy an annual cert from a reseller and forget the renewal email, HTTPS breaks on a Friday night. If you use ACME automation (host panel, Cloudflare, certbot, or docstoc), shortening is mostly invisible — the system reissues before expiry.

FAQ

Are shorter certificates less secure?

No — they reduce the usefulness of a leaked key. Security improves when issuance is automated correctly.

Will docstoc keep up if lifetimes shrink further?

docstoc’s model is ACME automation against Let's Encrypt. Shorter public DV lifetimes reinforce that approach rather than undermine it.
